\section{Denoising a cut with a suffix family}

The learner cannot test $q\in D_m$ directly, the single read $\mathrm{MQ}(x_q m)$ will have
signal only $s$. Each node thus uses a family of suffixes $V=(v_1,\dots,v_k)$ and
takes a vote over the $k$ noisy queries $\mathrm{MQ}(x_q m v_i)$.

Ideally the suffixes are accept-preserving (Def.~\ref{def:accept-preserving}), so
that querying a string $x$ against the family $V$ gives $k$ independent noisy reads
of the same bit $\mathbf 1[x\in L]$.

\begin{definition}[Vote and decision]\label{def:decide}
The vote of a string $x$ against the family is
\[
  \bar r(x)\;=\;\frac1k\sum_{i=1}^k \mathrm{MQ}(x\,v_i).
\]
Given a split point $\beta$ and an evidence margin $\tau$, the decision is
\[
  \mathrm{decide}_{\beta,\tau}(x)\;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    \textsc{accept} & \bar r(x)>\beta+\tau,\\
    \textsc{reject} & \bar r(x)<\beta-\tau,\\
    \textsc{indecisive} & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\]
A node with midfix $m$ classifies $u$ by $\mathrm{decide}_{\beta,\tau}(u\,m)$; the split
$\beta$ and margin $\tau$ are set by the clustering (\S\ref{subsec:clustering}), not
fixed in advance.
\end{definition}

If $v$ are all accept-preserving, the $k$ reads are independent with a common accept-probability
$p$, so $\bar r\sim\tfrac1k\mathrm{Bin}(k,p)$: a string with a definite side has
$p=\beta\pm s$ for the per-read signal $s$, one with no definite side has $p=\beta$.

\begin{definition}[fpr and fnr]\label{def:fpr-fnr}
We can define
\[
  \mathrm{fpr}=\Pr_{p=\beta}\!\big[\mathrm{decide}_{\beta,\tau}\in\{\textsc{accept},\textsc{reject}\}\big],
  \qquad
  \mathrm{fnr}=\Pr_{p=\beta+s}\!\big[\mathrm{decide}_{\beta,\tau}=\textsc{indecisive}\big].
\]
$\mathrm{fpr}$ upper-bounds the probability of a misclassification, while $\mathrm{fnr}$ is the
probability of abstaining on a definite-side string.
\end{definition}

The signal $s$ is the one model parameter; the split $\beta$ is estimated each round from
the cut's own separation (Alg.~\ref{alg:cluster}). Given $s$ and the current $\beta$, the
pair $(k,\tau)$ is set to meet target budgets $\mathrm{fpr}<\delta_{\mathrm{fpr}}$ and
$\mathrm{fnr}<\delta_{\mathrm{fnr}}$; the FNR budget is required of every prefix population
separately (\S\ref{subsec:populations}), not of their pooled average.

\subsection{Prefix populations}\label{subsec:populations}

The learner does not read its rates over one sample but over a collection of prefix
populations, each drawn a different way and each held to the rates on its own:
\begin{itemize}
\item the uniform pool $P_{\mathrm{unif}}$, sampled from the distribution $D$ the goal is
stated over;
\item for each state $q$ the current tree exposes, a population $P_q$ of the prefixes the
tree rests at $q$'s leaf;
\item for each round, a boundary population of the strings that round's tree could not
place decisively.
\end{itemize}
A prefix may lie in more than one. Keeping them apart is arithmetic: a rate over their
union is carried by whichever population is largest, so a family that reads one small
population entirely backwards moves the pooled rate by only that population's share and
hides there. Every rate below --- the clustering loss, the FNR gate, the certification
counts --- is therefore stated per population. A population the learner cannot fill (a
state no sampled string reaches) is dropped rather than held to a rate it has no prefixes
to report.

\subsection{The clustering algorithm}\label{subsec:clustering}

Reading each of $n$ representative prefixes against each candidate suffix fills a noisy
mask matrix $M[v][j]=\mathrm{MQ}(x_j\,v)\in\{0,1\}$, one row per candidate. The empty
suffix's row $M[\varepsilon][j]=\mathrm{MQ}(x_j)$ is a noisy read of the true membership
$\mathbf 1[x_j\in L]$. A family is a triple $(V,\beta,\tau)$: a size-$k$ cluster $V$ of
rows anchored at $\varepsilon$, the split $\beta$ estimated from its cut, and the
evidence margin $\tau$ the calibration then sets. The clustering (Alg.~\ref{alg:cluster})
produces $V$ and $\beta$; the loop (Alg.~\ref{alg:gate}) sets $\tau$ and grows the family
until every population is decided and the cut is certified accept-preserving.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{$\textsc{Cluster}(\beta)$: the $\varepsilon$-anchored family and a refreshed split}
\label{alg:cluster}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $w_j\gets 1/|P(j)|$ for each prefix $j$ \Comment{$P(j)$ its population: equal say per population, not per prefix}
\State $V\gets\{\varepsilon\}$,\quad $\ell^\star\gets\infty$
\Loop
  \State $c[j]\gets\mathbf 1[\mathrm{mean}_{v\in V}M[v][j]>\beta]$ \Comment{per-prefix centre}
  \State $N\gets$ the $k$ rows of least loss $\ell(v)=\sum_j w_j\,\mathbf 1[M[v][j]\ne c[j]]$
  \If{$\varepsilon\notin N$ \textbf{ or } $\textstyle\sum_{v\in N}\ell(v)\ge\ell^\star$} \textbf{break} \EndIf
  \State $V\gets N$,\quad $\ell^\star\gets\sum_{v\in N}\ell(v)$
\EndLoop
\State $\beta\gets\tfrac12\big(\overline r_{\mathrm{acc}}+\overline r_{\mathrm{rej}}\big)$ \Comment{midpoint of the mean vote on $V$'s two sides}
\State \textbf{return} $(V,\beta)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The stopping test keeps $\varepsilon$ from being evicted, not from being outvoted. A
coherent group agreeing with $\varepsilon$ on every state but one sits a hair from
$M[\varepsilon]$, so $\varepsilon$ stays a low-loss member while the other $k-1$ rows
cut that state the wrong way: the family is decisive, contains $\varepsilon$, and is not
accept-preserving. The weighting is the first defence: a state whose prefixes are a
percent of the union carries a percent of an unweighted loss, so a cut can invert it for
free; weighted, that state has the same say as the pool and a cut against it costs. Two
gates then read what the loss let through. The FNR gate holds each population to
$\delta_{\mathrm{fnr}}$ on its own and, when one reads high, grows that population --- the
lever that bears on the failure, since drawing more of a population it is not failing on
only re-weights the average. But a cut in the wrong class is perfectly decisive, so the
FNR gate passes it. The certification gate reads the cut back on $M[\varepsilon]$ ---
membership of $x_j$ itself --- and splits the judgement: any population may veto (it reads
as the class it is not), only the uniform pool may admit (it separates the two classes at
all), and admission takes positive evidence rather than absence of a veto.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{$\textsc{SampleFamily}$: the resampling loop and its gates}
\label{alg:gate}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Loop
  \State $(V,\beta)\gets\textsc{Cluster}(\beta)$;\quad $\tau\gets$ margin meeting the budgets at $(\beta,k)$
  \State $\widehat{\mathrm{fnr}}_P\gets$ fraction of $P$ left \textsc{indecisive} by $(V,\beta,\tau)$, each population $P$
  \State $P^\star\gets\arg\max_P\widehat{\mathrm{fnr}}_P$
  \If{$|V|<k$ \textbf{ or } $\widehat{\mathrm{fnr}}_{P^\star}>\delta_{\mathrm{fnr}}$}
     \State draw more suffixes, or grow $P^\star$; \textbf{continue}
  \EndIf
  \State on $M[\varepsilon]$, count each population's two sides of the cut
  \If{some population reads as the opposite class} \textbf{ refuse and resample} \Comment{veto}
  \ElsIf{$P_{\mathrm{unif}}$'s two sides both clear their class} \State \textbf{return} $(V,\beta,\tau)$ \Comment{admit}
  \Else{} draw more of $P_{\mathrm{unif}}$ for the split \Comment{undecided}
  \EndIf
\EndLoop
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Soundness of a decisive decision}

\begin{lemma}[A decisive decision matches the noiseless rule]\label{lem:decisive}
Any $(V,\beta)$ induces a noiseless rule on states,
\[
  \mathrm{decide}^\star(q)=\begin{cases}\textsc{accept}&\mu(q)>\beta,\\ \textsc{reject}&\mu(q)<\beta,\end{cases}
  \qquad \mu(q)=\mathbb E[\bar r(x_q\,m)],
\]
the true mean of the vote, a function of the state alone: a genuine
Myhill--Nerode predicate. A decisive noisy decision matches it,
$\mathrm{decide}(u\,m)=\mathrm{decide}^\star(q^\star(u))$, except with probability
$\le\mathrm{fpr}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Disagreeing --- say $\textsc{accept}$ while $\mu(q^\star(u))<\beta$ --- needs
$\bar r(u\,m)>\beta+\tau$, a deviation of at least $\tau$ above the
mean $\mu$, of probability $\le\mathrm{fpr}$ whatever $(V,\beta)$.
\end{proof}

A corollary is that soundness of \textsc{Accept}/\textsc{Reject} classifications
(i.e., they reflect genuinely different classes) is guaranteed for any $(V,\beta)$.
The choice of $(V,\beta)$ only affects coverage: how many prefixes are classified
decisively, and how many are left \textsc{indecisive}. The gate holds the decided
fraction $1-\widehat{\mathrm{fnr}}_P(V)$ to $\ge1-\delta_{\mathrm{fnr}}$ on every
population $P$, not merely on their pooled average, so no population is decided by proxy
for another.

\subsection{Convergence}

Correctness does not rest on the clustering. As \S\ref{subsec:clustering} notes, the
$\varepsilon$-anchored cluster can settle --- decisively, with $\varepsilon$ retained
--- on a family that cuts one state against the language, and neither the anchoring nor
the FNR gate sees it. What rules such a family out is the certification gate: the
returned family is accept-preserving because it was certified so, not because the
cluster found the right rows and not because a test failed to disprove it.

\begin{lemma}[Certifying the cut, population by population]\label{lem:certify}
Read the empty-suffix column $M[\varepsilon]$ over each population's two sides of the cut;
where a side of population $P$ holds $n$ prefixes, $h$ of them reading $1$, one-sided
binomial tests at level $\alpha$ apply. A population is read as the opposite class ---
a veto --- when its accept side sits below $\beta+\tau$ or its reject side above
$\beta-\tau$,
\[
   \Pr[\mathrm{Bin}(n_A,\beta+\tau)\le h_A]\le\alpha
   \quad\text{or}\quad
   \Pr[\mathrm{Bin}(n_R,\beta-\tau)\ge h_R]\le\alpha ,
\]
the level shared across the sides read. Any population may veto: a state whose prefixes
all read backwards is disproof of accept-preservation whatever the pooled rate does. The
uniform pool admits, and only it, when both its sides clear their own class,
\[
   \Pr[\mathrm{Bin}(n_A,\beta+\tau)\ge h_A]\le\alpha
   \quad\text{and}\quad
   \Pr[\mathrm{Bin}(n_R,\beta-\tau)\le h_R]\le\alpha .
\]
Only $P_{\mathrm{unif}}$ can, because admission is the claim that the family separates the
two classes at all, and only a sample drawn at the oracle's base rate straddles both; a
state's prefixes are one class and can veto but cannot attest to a separation they never
straddle. The gate returns the family only when $P_{\mathrm{unif}}$ admits and no
population vetoes. Writing $f$ for the share of an admitted side that is really the other
class, its accept-side rate is $\beta+s-2sf$, so admission certifies $f<(s-\tau)/2s$ on
that side with confidence $\ge1-\alpha$. Absence of a veto is not admission: a family the
uniform pool leaves undecided is resampled with more uniform reads, never returned.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Were an admitted accept side's true rate $\le\beta+\tau$ --- drift at or past the bar ---
then $h_A$ is stochastically below $\mathrm{Bin}(n_A,\beta+\tau)$, whose upper tail
exceeds $\alpha$ except with probability $\le\alpha$; likewise the reject side. So
admission holds only for a cut whose two sides sit past $\beta\pm\tau$, which by
$\text{rate}=\beta+s-2sf$ is $f<(s-\tau)/2s$. The veto is the same test read the other
way: a side sitting on the wrong side of its threshold past $\alpha$ is a population the
oracle contradicts. A family the counts place on neither side of the bar triggers neither
test and is resampled.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The gate returns a certified family]\label{thm:cut}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:preserve} the loop halts with probability $1$, and the family
it returns decides at least $1-\delta_{\mathrm{fnr}}$ of every population and is admitted
by the uniform pool with no population vetoing (Lemma~\ref{lem:certify}): each side of its
cut is a share $f<(s-\tau)/2s$ of the other class, with confidence $\ge1-\alpha$. When no
accept-preserving family exists the uniform pool never admits and the idealized loop does
not halt.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The clustering proposes an $\varepsilon$-anchored family each round; the gate returns it
only when every population is decided to $\delta_{\mathrm{fnr}}$, the uniform pool admits,
and none vetoes, so the returned family carries those guarantees whatever the proposer
did. It remains to see the loop halts under Assumption~\ref{ass:preserve}. Screened suffix
draws are accept-preserving with probability $\ge p_{\mathrm{preserve}}$; growing the
worst population supplies the reads a state's rate needs rather than diluting it with a
population that already passes; and as the pool and per-state populations grow the screen
drops any suffix flipping a state on positive $D$-mass, so the candidate pool is
eventually dominated by accept-preserving rows and the cluster proposes a driftless
family. A driftless family decides each population and draws no veto, and the uniform pool
admits it once its minority side is read out, so the loop returns.
\end{proof}

When no accept-preserving family exists this loop runs forever; the implementation caps
it with a give-up budget, spent only on families a population has vetoed (established
drift, not mere uncertainty), and then reports the target has none.

The errors the per-population statement removes are both a rate standing in for a
population it does not describe. A pooled FNR is carried by the largest population, so a
family that leaves one state entirely indecisive --- or cuts it entirely backwards ---
clears a pooled limit that the state's prefixes are a few percent of; stated per
population the state reads $1$ and the gate grows that state until the family resolves it.
And admission read off a pooled column moves only by a drifted population's share: a
family whose accept side was $69\%$ mislabelled cleared a $10^{-3}$ threshold at
$1.1\times10^{-3}$, because the aggregate barely moves under gross drift when the drifted
class is thinly observed. Splitting the judgement fixes both --- any population may veto,
only the base-rate pool may admit, and each is held to the FNR on its own --- and
admission still takes positive evidence, so the gate fails closed. Soundness of the
decisive labels (Lemma~\ref{lem:decisive}) is, as before, free of all of it.
